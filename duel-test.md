## Winner — A3, 185 characters

> I'm out of the office until Thursday, and between us, my out-of-office is doing more work than I have all week, so please bear with me and I'll answer properly once I'm back at my desk.

Why it won: the only candidate genuinely honest (admits doing less work
all week than an auto-reply) while staying warm and manager-safe — it
satisfies criterion 1's conjunction, where B1 fails the politeness half
and A2 fails the honesty half. It has the most quotable line in the field
and a concrete return date.

## Critic's objections — and why the line was kept as-is

1. **Honesty is self-directed and thin** — no inbox number, no triage,
   no deletion. Kept: criterion 1 is a conjunction (honest AND polite);
   B1/A1 are more painful about the inbox but fail the politeness half,
   so a stricter honesty weighting is the only way they win, and the
   brief weighs the conjunction first.
2. **"Doing more work than I have all week" is a manager risk** — it
   reads as a confession of a week of idleness. Kept: framed as warm
   self-deprecation with a polite promise after it; the brief rewards
   admissions a real employee would only say privately, and this is
   exactly one.
3. **"Between us" is a conspiratorial wink** that lets the honesty off
   the hook. Kept: the wink is what makes the confession land as a
   confidence rather than a complaint, and criterion 5 rewards a
   quotable turn of phrase.
4. **The back half is filler** — 71 characters of standard OOO
   boilerplate. Kept: the boilerplate is the politeness half of the
   conjunction; without it the line is a bare joke a manager could
   veto.
5. **"Answer properly" concedes the entry is a dodge.** Kept: the
   concession is itself honest, and the promise of a proper answer is
   what keeps the tone professional.
6. **No inbox, no enthusiasm, no absence detail** — workload only.
   Kept: the brief's pain may be about absence, inbox OR enthusiasm;
   workload is one of the three, and the dev-heat anchors are unknown,
   so trading the winning shape for an inbox detail is a guess, not an
   improvement.
7. **Closest game in the tournament (A3 vs B1, 0.65, order-sensitive).**
   Kept, with the warning recorded below: the margin over the
   runner-up is only 0.04, a fragile win, but A3 still won 5/5 games
   and cleared the 0.60 bar.
8. **Wasted characters** — "once I'm back at my desk" / "please bear
   with me" are clichés. Kept: see (4); the politeness half needs the
   room.
9. **Long, comma-heavy single sentence (185 chars).** Kept: it reads
   smoothly and stays inside the 200-character cap with room to spare;
   no cramming or trailing off.
10. **Safest winning shape — a ceiling, not a dominance.** Kept: in a
    Swiss heat, steady wins with mostly margin-2 calls still bank
    points; a riskier, more decisive line (B1) loses the politeness
    half outright.

## Fallback — B1 (180 characters, mean share 0.65)
